As I started doing some research, I found that one of the most important variables in shaving is the space between the leading edge of the razor and the guard protecting it-what we refer to as the blade gap.

See the image below...

Too much space meant that cutting yourself was inevitable. Too little space meant that it was nearly impossible to cut anything.

The next time you look at a cartridge razor, notice how much more space is in front of the first blade in the cartridge compared with the spacing between each additional blade. And when I spoke with design engineers about how these things really work, my findings were quite shocking.

It turns out, in the cartridge systems we tested, the first blade is the one doing the actual cutting.

So... what are the other blades really for?

What did we find?

Well, after a year of research, we learned that there are essentially 3 "secret ingredients," critical in delivering a great shave.

I'm going to share our secrets right here. Do you think the biggest players in the shaving world would ever share their trade secrets?

But as you'll see, my approach to everything about shaving is the polar opposite of these big corporate brands... so here goes:

SECRET #1. The angle of attack.

The angle of the blade when it hits your skin is critical. Too steep, and it'll cut your face. Too shallow, and it won't cut hair effectively. We ran more than 100 tests simply to get the blade angle exactly right. See the image below. WE discovered an optimal angle of 31.3 degrees.

SECRET #2: Exposure

The second critical component of any razor is the exposure of the blade. In other words, how much of the blade actually presses against your face.

Again, too much can be VERY dangerous... but you need just the right amount to cut effectively. And again, we ran hundreds of tests to get the optimal exposure, which we found to be 0.15 millimeters.

SECRET #3. Gap

The third critical feature on any razor is the blade gap.

In other words, the chasm between the blade and the body of the razor. It's a matter of painstaking trial and error to get exactly the right gap, so skin and hair fill the gap, in just the right amount, before passing the blade.

We found the exact right gap to be 0.65 millimeters.

With all of these metrics... angle, exposure, and blade gap, our designers made tiny tweaks... down to 4/1000th of an inch... until we settled on the perfect formula.

These numbers are the secrets for delivering an incredible shave. And what we found is that all it takes is one single, fantastic blade... rather than three... or four... or even five cheap ones.

Along the way we learned some other important secrets too.

For example...

We learned that some innovations in cartridge razors are actually quite good–and make a big difference.

One of these innovations is the "pivoting head." This does, in fact, help give you a better, closer, and safer shave.

Although some shaving enthusiasts scoff at this idea, we found in test after test after test that a pivoting head helps deliver a world-class shave—because it helps you maintain the ideal angle, with hardly any experience or practice.

The thing you have to keep in mind is: We had no biases going into this search for the world's perfect shave.

We just wanted to figure out what works and what doesn't.

For example, even though it adds a lot to the cost, sophistication, and intricacy of the design... we designed a razor with a pivoting head.

Inside the handle, there's a tiny joint between the razor head (which holds the blade) and the handle, which enables just the right amount of pivot.

There's also a tiny Teflon coated, stainless steel spring that regulates this joint and thus allows the head to "float" on your skin. This controls the amount of pressure you can apply against your face when you're shaving.

Again, we approached this project with no preconceived notions... and no biases. We were simply in pursuit of the world's best shave... something the average guy like you or me could do in our own homes, without a lot of practice.

The Leading Edge Problem

Tod literally traveled to the ends of the earth in search of the absolute world's best shaving blade to fit our razor.

He met with manufacturers of meat processing blades, medical scalpels, food processing blades, and more. He traveled all around the globe: The U.S.... Germany... China... Japan.

We investigated and tested blades of various materials... from steel (stainless and high carbon alloys), to ceramic, tungsten carbide, and amorphous metals. One of our early ideas was to perfect a ceramic blade, which in theory would last for 30 days.

We tested various forms of blade coatings, from Teflon, Ceramic, and Boron carbides.

We knew that to deliver the world's finest shave, we would need the highest-quality blade available anywhere.

We knew manufacturing our own blades would be expensive. But I didn't care about the expense. The real issue we ran into is what we now call the "leading edge" problem.

It turns out that sharpness is not the key to a world-class shave. Of course, the edge needs to be sharp... really sharp.

But more importantly, to deliver a great shave, a blade needs to be flawless. The leading edge has to be so smooth that you can only see it with a microscope.

Look at these two pictures from one of our manufacturing partners.

You'll notice that one of these blades has an edge with a perfectly uniform appearance... while the other has hundreds of "striations," or minor imperfections.

These tiny imperfections–far too small to see with the naked eye–cause the blade to "burn" against the skin.

It turns out that one of the key secrets to a really comfortable shave is a PERFECT leading edge.

And few people around the world can deliver perfect leading edges in volume.

Sure, you can "strop" a blade–that's the official term for polishing and sharpening a blade... as you'll see many barbers and chefs do. But it's not feasible to hand strop thousands of blades each week.
